The framework

Five questions that cover most of chess strategy.

Every opening and middlegame plan above is really just an answer to one of these. When you don't know what to play, come back to this list.

01

Where's the king?

An uncastled or exposed king is worth more than a pawn. Before any other plan, check whether either king is a target right now.

02

Who controls the center?

Pieces reach more of the board from central squares. A knight on the rim is famously dim for a reason — count who has more central space and influence.

03

Is every piece doing something?

A piece stuck on its home square is a piece you're playing without. Development isn't just the opening — a bishop biting on granite in the middlegame is still undeveloped.

04

What do the pawns say?

Doubled, isolated, and backward pawns are long-term weaknesses; passed pawns are long-term assets. Pawn structure usually outlives whatever piece maneuver you're considering.

05

Whose plan is faster?

When both sides are attacking on opposite wings, the position is a race. Count the moves each side needs — not just whether the plan is good.
